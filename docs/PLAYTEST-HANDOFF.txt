LAUNCH ON YOUR LINUX DESKTOP — no server commands or build tools needed

Save the archive and its .sha256 sidecar together in a writable local folder.
Open a desktop terminal there, then:

    sha256sum -c breakwater-linux-x86_64.tar.gz.sha256
    tar -xzf breakwater-linux-x86_64.tar.gz
    cd breakwater-linux-x86_64
    ./launch.sh

Checksum must say OK; do not run a mismatching download. Alternatively compare
sha256sum breakwater-linux-x86_64.tar.gz to the full digest above.
The first launch imports bundled assets; give it a moment. The extracted folder
must be writable and permit executable files. Normal play is offline; the bundled
Godot 4.5.1 runtime needs no Godot, Python or Git installation.

GOAL AND CONTROLS

Destroy all THREE orange pylons, then enter the north EXTRACT zone alive before
150 active seconds expire. All three destroyed unlocks extraction; you must still
enter it. Sentries threaten you: move, fire, dash and use cover. Pause stops time.

    WASD                move
    Mouse               aim
    Hold left mouse     fire
    Space               dash (cooldown on HUD)
    Esc                 pause/resume and sound settings
    R                   retry after victory/defeat, not during active play
    M                   mute/unmute
    - / =               decrease/increase SFX level
    Settings sliders    Master and SFX volume
    Close window/Alt+F4  quit

No save/resume. R rebuilds the session; sound preferences persist.

PREREQUISITES AND TROUBLESHOOTING

Linux x86_64/glibc, modern SSE4.2-capable CPU, X11 or XWayland, and working
OpenGL 3.3 drivers. Launcher explicitly uses X11/OpenGL compatibility, not Vulkan.
Allow at least 500 MB for extraction/import. Desktop audio needs PulseAudio,
PipeWire with pulse compatibility, or ALSA plus a working output device.
Ubuntu/Pop!_OS libraries: libgl1, libgl1-mesa-dri, libx11-6, libxcursor1,
libxrandr2, libxinerama1, libxi6, libxrender1, libxext6, libpulse0, and
libasound2t64 (libasound2 on older Ubuntu). Most desktops already have these;
if a library is missing, send G the exact error rather than doing server work.

Missing display: launch from your graphical desktop; XWayland must be available.
Permission denied: extract on a writable/executable Linux filesystem; file modes
are present in this tar archive. No sound: check M, sliders and desktop output;
optional diagnostic: ./launch.sh --audio-driver PulseAudio (or ALSA).
Driver/black-window issue: try LIBGL_ALWAYS_SOFTWARE=1 ./launch.sh as a slower
diagnostic. Log a problem with ./launch.sh --verbose > launch.log 2>&1 and send
the log plus your distro/GPU. More detail and licenses: extracted README.txt.

SHORT PLAYTEST — Rick’s judgment is still needed (node 15)

Try a full run, retry with R after a win/loss, and try again.
  1. Destruction: do pieces launch along distinct trajectories, spin, fall under
     gravity, hit the ground and bounce/settle convincingly rather than vanish?
  2. Sound: are breaks and ground impacts audible, varied and satisfying at your
     speakers/headphones? Any silence, clipping, repetition or excessive volume?
  3. Controls: movement, aim/fire, dash, pause/resume, mute/volume, retry and quit.
     Any missed input, stuck controls, lag, or stale state after retry?
  4. Readability: can you track player, pylons, threats, debris, health, dash,
     timer and locked/open extraction without guessing or losing the action?
  5. Threat/tension/fun: do enemies matter, is there a reason to move/use cover,
     does the deadline add tension, and do you want another run? What falls flat?

Reply with your distro/GPU, whether it launched and made sound, whether you won
or lost, your verdict on those five areas, and specific blockers/changes wanted.
An honest “not fun” is useful. No human verdict has been assumed or requested by
this worker; this is the prepared request for the controller’s later human gate.

LIMITS — technical evidence is not a human playtest

Only Linux x86_64/glibc (server glibc 2.43) with X11/OpenGL, Xvfb and Mesa llvmpipe
was server-tested. Rick’s hardware, physical keyboard/mouse, GPUs, other distros
and older glibc remain unverified. Native Wayland, ARM, musl, 32-bit and Windows
are not supported/tested release targets. No universal Linux promise is made.
Desktop speakers were NOT tested: node 8 had no PulseAudio/ALSA device/session.
Actual mixer PCM checks are NOT subjective listening or proof of speaker output.
Node 8 measured mean real-time 35.50 FPS with observer overhead on software GL;
its offline 60 FPS capture is not a real-time performance claim. Automated pixel
sampling proves rendering/bindings, not visual satisfaction/readability. Sound
quality, control feel, threat, tension and fun remain Rick’s node-15 judgment.
No nodes 9, 15 or 0 are declared complete by this work.
